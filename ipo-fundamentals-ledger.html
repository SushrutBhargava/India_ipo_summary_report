What do these mean
Financial Health, what each metric measures
ROE (Return on Equity) Net Profit / Shareholders' Equity

How much profit the company generates for every rupee shareholders have invested. Higher is generally better; compare against the sector, not in isolation.

ROCE (Return on Capital Employed) EBIT / (Total Assets - Current Liabilities)

Return on all capital deployed in the business, both debt and equity. A better read than ROE alone for capital-intensive or leveraged businesses.

RoNW (Return on Net Worth) PAT / Net Worth

Similar to ROE; SEBI disclosures typically report this figure directly in the restated financial statements.

D/E (Debt-to-Equity) Total Debt / Shareholders' Equity

How leveraged the company is. Zero debt renders as "0.00 (zero debt)" in the tables below. Rising D/E year over year is a leverage flag worth weighing against the issue's stated use of proceeds.

PAT Margin Net Profit / Revenue

How much of every rupee of revenue converts to bottom-line profit. Thin margins on a large revenue base signal a low-margin, high-volume business model.

EBITDA Margin EBITDA / Revenue

Operating profitability before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation; strips out capital-structure and accounting effects to compare core operating performance.

How the Health tag is set: each card's "Health: Strong / Mixed / Caution" tag comes from the 3-year revenue and PAT trend (growing, flat, or declining) combined with the D/E trajectory, read together rather than off any single ratio.

Valuation, what each metric measures
EPS (Earnings Per Share) Net Profit / Shares Outstanding

Shown pre and post issue in the Valuation & GMP table; post-issue EPS accounts for the new shares created by the fresh issue, so it is typically lower than pre-issue EPS.

P/E (Price-to-Earnings) Issue Price / EPS

What multiple of earnings an investor pays at the issue price. Shown pre and post issue; the post-issue figure is the more relevant one since it reflects the diluted share base after listing.

P/BV (Price-to-Book Value) Issue Price / Book Value per Share

What multiple of net asset value an investor pays. A P/BV above 1 means the market is pricing in future earnings power beyond the company's stated net worth.

Peer comparison Verbatim from RHP only

A peer multiple is cited only when that specific issue's own RHP discloses a "Comparison with Listed Industry Peers" table. No peer set is researched or guessed; where none is disclosed, the multiple is presented as a standalone read.

How the Valuation tag is set: "Attractive / Fair / Rich" reflects where the post-issue P/E (and P/BV) sits, benchmarked against the disclosed peer set when one exists, or read on its own merits when it does not.

Fund Usage, what each term means
Fresh Issue New shares, new capital

New shares are created and sold; the money raised goes directly to the company's own balance sheet to fund whatever the prospectus names (capex, debt repayment, working capital, acquisitions).

OFS (Offer for Sale) Existing shares, no new capital to the company

Existing shareholders (promoters, early investors) sell part of their own holding. The proceeds go to those sellers, not to the company; a pure-OFS issue raises no fresh capital for the business at all.

Objects of the Issue From the DRHP, verbatim

The specific line items the fresh-issue proceeds are earmarked for: growth capex, debt repayment, working capital, or general corporate purposes. Sourced from each prospectus's own "Objects of the Issue" section.

How the Fund Use tag is set: "Growth-led" means proceeds go mainly to expansion or capex; "Debt-heavy" means mainly loan repayment; "OFS-only" means no fresh capital reaches the company at all; "Mixed" spans more than one of these.

Subscription, what each term means
Overall subscription Shares bid for / Shares on offer

The headline multiple, e.g. "3.00x" means bids came in for three times the shares actually on offer, across all investor categories combined.

QIB (Qualified Institutional Buyers) Mutual funds, banks, insurers, FIIs

Large institutional investors. QIB demand is often read as the most informed vote of confidence, since these are professional investors with deeper access to management and diligence.

NII (Non-Institutional Investors) HNIs bidding above the retail limit

High-net-worth individuals and corporates bidding above the retail cap. Often the most leverage-sensitive and sentiment-driven category.

Retail Individual investors, up to the retail limit

Individual applicants bidding within the retail investment cap. Broad retail enthusiasm does not by itself confirm institutional conviction; the QIB read matters more for fundamentals-driven demand.

How the Subscription tag is set: "Very strong" and "Oversubscribed" reflect high overall and QIB multiples; "Building - Day N" marks a book still filling during bidding; "Undersubscribed" or "Not opened" mark weak or pre-bidding states.
